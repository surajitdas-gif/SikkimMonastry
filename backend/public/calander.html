Cultural Calendar

Monastery Festivals Calendar

Important Note: Most of these festivals follow the Tibetan Lunisolar Calendar, so the dates change every year. The schedule below is based on typical timings. Always confirm the exact dates with local authorities before planning your visit.

Upcoming Festivals (Late 2025)

NOV
Late

Lhabab Duchen

Commemorates Lord Buddha's descent from Heaven after teaching Dharma to his mother. It is a highly auspicious day where the effects of all actions are believed to be multiplied.

Key Monasteries: Rumtek, Enchey, and Pemayangtse.
Book Now
DEC
Late

Kagyed Dance (Chaam)

A vibrant masked dance festival symbolizing the destruction of evil forces. Performed by monks, it invokes protector deities to welcome peace and prosperity for the new year.

Key Monasteries: Rumtek (most famous), Phodong, Enchey.
Book Now

Major Annual Festivals

FEB-MAR

Losar (Tibetan New Year)

The most important festival in the region, celebrating the Tibetan New Year with prayers, feasts, and traditional performances to welcome a prosperous year ahead.

Key Monasteries: Celebrated in all monasteries across Sikkim.
Book Now
FEB-MAR

Bumchu Festival

A unique sacred festival where a pot of holy water is opened after a year. The water level is believed to predict the fortune for the coming year for all of Sikkim.

Key Monasteries: Exclusively at Tashiding Monastery, West Sikkim.
Book Now
MAY-JUN

Saga Dawa

The holiest Buddhist month, celebrating the birth, enlightenment, and passing of Lord Buddha. A grand procession of monks carrying holy texts is a major highlight in Gangtok.

Key Monasteries: All monasteries. Main procession from Tsuklakhang Palace.
Book Now
JUL-AUG

Drukpa Tshechi

This festival celebrates Lord Buddha's first sermon on the Four Noble Truths, which he delivered to his first five disciples at the Deer Park in Sarnath.

Key Monasteries: Observed in monasteries throughout the state, including Rumtek.
Book Now
AUG-SEP

Pang Lhabsol

A festival unique to Sikkim that worships Mount Kanchenjunga as the guardian deity. It features the spectacular 'Pangtoed Chaam' warrior dance.

Key Monasteries: Grandest celebration at Tsuklakhang Palace, Gangtok.
Book Now
