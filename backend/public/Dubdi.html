Dubdi Monastery

The oldest monastery in Sikkim, located near Yuksom, founded in the 17th century.

Dubdi Monastery, also known as the “Hermit’s Cell,” was established in 1701 by Lhatsun Namkha Jigme, a revered Nyingma Buddhist Lama. It is the oldest monastery in Sikkim and played a key role in the coronation of the first Chogyal (king) of Sikkim. The monastery is perched on a hilltop surrounded by lush forest and provides panoramic views of the surrounding valleys.

Key Features

  • Oldest monastery in Sikkim, founded in 1701.
  • Built in traditional Nyingma Buddhist architectural style.
  • Houses sacred relics, statues, and ancient scriptures.
  • Famous for its serene location and panoramic views of the Khangchendzonga range.
  • Integral to Sikkimese history, including the coronation of the first Chogyal.

History & Significance

Dubdi Monastery was established by Lama Lhatsun Namkha Jigme and has been a center for meditation and Buddhist study for over three centuries. It is spiritually significant as the first monastery of Sikkim and continues to attract devotees and historians interested in Sikkim’s religious and cultural heritage.

Tourist & Travel Information

Dubdi is accessible via a short trek from Yuksom, which is the starting point for the Goecha La trek. Visitors can explore the monastery, enjoy the scenic hilltop surroundings, and witness traditional rituals and ceremonies performed by resident monks.
